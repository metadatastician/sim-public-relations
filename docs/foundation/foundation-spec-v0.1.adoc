= PR Practice Lab Foundation Specification v0.1
:toc: left
:reproducible:

== Purpose

PR Practice Lab is an evidence-driven, time-aware practice simulator for
developing and assessing professional judgement in fictional public-relations
and communications work. Its priority user is a person blocked from a first
role or career transition because job advertisements ask for prior practical
experience.

It creates structured opportunities to perform realistic work, receive
evidence-linked feedback and assemble a transparent simulated-practice record.
It is not employment, live client work, supervised experience, a professional
qualification, a promise of competence or employment, or accredited CPD.

== Design response to AI displacement

AI can perform more of the routine production that once gave beginners a low-
risk way to learn. The simulator therefore treats content generation as one
component of a larger professional system. It exercises the capabilities that
remain necessary when a first draft is cheap: problem framing, source testing,
stakeholder understanding, objectives, judgement, relationships, approval,
ethical resistance, verification, measurement, adaptation and accountability.

AI use is observable practice. A scenario declares whether it is prohibited,
permitted or required for each task. A learner records the system used, the
material supplied, the purpose, substantive interventions and verification.
The learner remains responsible for the result.

== Product invariants

. Hidden authored reality is immutable during a run and never enters an
  untrusted learner client or learner-controlled AI context.
. Learner-visible disclosure is monotonic: an item may be corrected,
  contradicted or superseded, but its historical availability is not erased.
. Fact, source, disclosure, interpretation, assumption, uncertainty,
  recommendation and approved message are distinct objects.
. Commands are attempts; only validated events change authoritative state.
. The event record is deterministic and portable for a recorded scenario seed.
. The learner can inspect what data is recorded and export or delete local runs.
. Assessment cites observable events and artefact revisions, not personality,
  accent, social background, personal following or outcome alone.
. Campaign outcome, client satisfaction and learner competence are reported
  separately.
. More than one route may be defensible on the information then available.
. Accessibility is a design and assessment concern, not a cosmetic channel
  adaptation at the end.
. AI assistance never converts unverified material into evidence and never
  removes human accountability.
. A portfolio export is visibly marked as fictional simulated practice.

== Phase A capability

A learner opens `northstar-mobility-001`, accepts a bounded junior agency role,
checks the brief, investigates the organisation and issue, maps stakeholders,
builds a working media/contact record, defines objectives and measures, proposes
a channel plan, drafts and revises materials, manages approvals, responds to an
enquiry, records AI use where applicable, monitors fictional response and
recommends whether to proceed, pause or change course.

Tasks arrive through realistic work artefacts: email, meeting note, chat,
research extract, monitoring item, draft, approval comment and stakeholder
response. Workstreams continue concurrently. Deadlines and changing evidence
force prioritisation; the learner cannot investigate or perfect everything.

== Practice ladder

Phase A supports four overlapping levels rather than job-title cosplay:

. `supported-production`: accurate research, monitoring, records, drafts,
  formatting, accessibility and version discipline.
. `coordinated-delivery`: brief clarification, schedules, approvals, channel
  adaptation, contact handling and status communication.
. `bounded-recommendation`: audience, objective, option, risk, measurement and
  escalation recommendations with reasons.
. `integrated-response`: reprioritisation when facts, stakeholder response or
  organisational decisions change.

A beginner may practise an advanced situation with support; completing it does
not confer a job title or professional status.

== Learning model

The learning unit is a traceable act:

----
available evidence -> interpretation + uncertainty -> objective and audience
-> considered options -> approved action -> response -> adaptation -> reflection
----

Assessment observes brief hygiene, research, source evaluation, audience and
stakeholder reasoning, planning, content judgement, editorial control,
relationships, delivery, measurement, ethics, AI accountability, collaboration,
prioritisation and reflection. Outcomes are consequences, not substitutes for
evidence of reasoning.

== Assessment and feedback

Practice mode may offer staged prompts. Assessment mode offers accessibility
and process affordances but no correctness hints before submission. Debrief
reveals the authored chronology, missed or contradicted evidence, response
logic, alternative defensible routes and rubric observations.

Critical errors remain separate from an aggregate result. Fabricating evidence,
inventing public support, concealing representation, knowingly publishing a
material falsehood, exposing protected personal data, bypassing a required
approval, or disguising prohibited AI use cannot be averaged away by polished
writing.

== Evidence passport boundary

The export binds selected artefacts and observations to a scenario version,
rule-pack version and event-record digest. It reports only what was observed in
the simulation. Hours are `simulation elapsed time`, never employment,
placement, client-service or accredited-practice hours.

== Professional references

The initial UK reference pack draws provisionally on first-party CIPR, PRCA and
AMEC materials recorded in `docs/rules/authority-registry.adoc`. References
guide scenario authorship and debriefs but cannot support authoritative scoring
until versioned interpretations are reviewed by competent practitioners.

== Privacy, safety and inclusion

All organisations, people, journalists, stakeholder groups, publications and
messages in authored scenarios are fictional. The reference product requires
no live contacts, social posting, scraping, media databases or external AI.
Learners do not impersonate real people or contact real organisations.

The product must support keyboard use, screen-reader structure, captions or
transcripts, adjustable time pressure, plain-language instructions and a
documented accommodation mode. Assessment must distinguish a supported access
need from the capability being assessed.

== Acceptance

This specification is satisfied only by executable evidence: deterministic
replay, branch fixtures, scenario validation, hidden-truth leak canaries,
accessible interaction tests, assessment calibration and honest capability
reporting. Missing integrations or professional review must fail loudly or be
reported as provisional; they must never be presented as complete.
