= Assessment Rubric v0.1
:toc: left

== Scale

Each competency is reported as `not-observed`, `developing`, `threshold` or
`strong`, with cited evidence. `Not-observed` means the run did not yield enough
evidence; it is not a failing personality judgement. Critical errors are
reported separately and cannot be averaged away.

== Route classes

`competent-standard`::
Proportionate investigation, clear objectives, audience-fit work, managed
approval, transparent measurement, timely adaptation and adequate record.

`competent-alternative`::
A different channel, sequence, launch recommendation or response that remains
defensible on the evidence then available and mitigates its distinct risks.

`minimally-acceptable`::
Usable threshold work with limited depth, no material deception and no critical
omission.

`common-error`::
Weak assumption, activity metric presented without context, avoidable delay,
over-broad media list, premature drafting, incomplete approval or poor handover
that does not itself make the work unsafe.

`critical-error`::
Fabricating evidence or a quotation; manufacturing or exaggerating public
support; concealing who is represented; knowingly publishing a material
falsehood; exposing protected personal data; impersonating a real person;
bypassing a required safety/legal approval; hiding a material conflict; or
disguising prohibited AI use.

== Threshold for the Phase A scenario

`threshold-demonstrated` requires threshold evidence in role/brief, research,
source/claim evaluation, stakeholders/audiences, objectives/planning, editorial
control, delivery/coordination, measurement, ethics and AI/data accountability;
no critical error; final submission; and a valid event record.

The remaining competencies contribute feedback and may support a higher result,
but the run cannot invent evidence for a capability the scenario did not test.

== Separate reports

The simulator reports four independent views:

. `practice evidence`: cited competency observations.
. `work outcome`: fictional reach, response, relationship and organisational
  consequences.
. `process context`: support level, AI condition, accommodation, retries and
  resource use.
. `integrity`: scenario/rule versions, event digest and critical errors.

A strong campaign outcome does not prove competence. A defensible decision to
pause a launch can demonstrate competence even when fictional output counts are
low.

== AI conditions

AI use neither raises nor lowers a score by itself. Assessment observes whether
use complied with policy, protected data, preserved provenance, improved the
work, and received adequate human verification. Undeclared or invented AI
citations are evaluated by their professional consequence, not by a blanket
anti-tool penalty.
