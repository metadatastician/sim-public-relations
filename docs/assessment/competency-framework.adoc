= Competency Framework
:toc: left

[cols="2,4",options="header"]
|===
|Competency |Observable evidence

|Role, brief and authority
|Clarifies purpose, scope, decision rights, deadlines, deliverables, constraints
and approval; records what remains unclear.

|Research and listening
|Uses proportionate sources, monitoring and direct evidence; distinguishes
signal from noise; records provenance and limitations.

|Source and claim evaluation
|Separates fact, interpretation, allegation, estimate and approved claim;
triangulates material assertions and corrects the record.

|Organisational understanding
|Connects communications work to organisational purpose, operating reality,
risk and the limits of what communication can achieve.

|Stakeholders, publics and audiences
|Identifies affected people as well as target audiences; considers interests,
power, access needs, trust, culture and possible unintended effects.

|Objectives and planning
|Defines specific communication objectives, baselines, strategy, activities,
responsibilities, dependencies, timing and meaningful measures.

|Channel and format judgement
|Selects earned, owned, shared, paid or interpersonal routes for a reason;
adapts form without fragmenting the underlying truth.

|Writing and editorial control
|Produces accurate, clear, concise, audience-appropriate and accessible work;
edits for meaning, tone, structure, evidence and house requirements.

|Visual, audio and digital literacy
|Briefs and checks non-text assets, metadata, accessibility, platform context,
consent and likely distribution effects.

|Media and intermediary relations
|Builds relevant records, understands intermediary needs, pitches honestly,
handles enquiries, keeps commitments and protects editorial independence.

|Stakeholder relationships
|Listens, responds respectfully, avoids extractive engagement and records how
stakeholder input affects decisions.

|Delivery and coordination
|Prioritises concurrent work, manages versions and approvals, communicates
status, preserves handover context and escalates blockers.

|Issues, risk and change
|Recognises emerging issues, calibrates urgency, prepares options, updates
holding material and adapts activity as evidence changes.

|Measurement and evaluation
|Links measures to objectives; distinguishes activities, outputs, out-takes,
outcomes and impact; avoids vanity or invented metrics; states attribution
limits.

|Ethics and public interest
|Represents identity and support honestly, resists deception, identifies
conflicts and affected publics, respects confidentiality and escalates unsafe,
misleading or unlawful requests.

|AI and data accountability
|Follows task policy, minimises supplied data, declares material AI use, checks
sources and claims, detects fabrication or bias and owns the final decision.

|Collaboration and counsel
|Invites relevant expertise, handles disagreement constructively, gives clear
recommendations and records dissent or unresolved risk.

|Reflection and transfer
|Explains reasons, limitations and consequences; identifies improvements and
adapts learning to a materially different context.
|===

Each observation cites events and artefact versions with `supporting`,
`contrary` or `insufficient` polarity. Outcomes and prose polish cannot alone
establish a competency.
